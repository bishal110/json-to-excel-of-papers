## SECTION 2: OPERATIONAL PROBLEM


**What practical field problem is being solved?:** Human surveillance must combine surface measurements, downhole conditions and component design limits; isolated alarms cannot identify all developing operating risks.

**What production losses are associated with this problem?:** Lost lift, falling throughput, premature equipment replacement and shutdown; no quantified production loss or benefit.

**How would an offshore production engineer recognize this problem?:** Compare measured/calculated conditions with pump, motor and cable ratings and investigate low intake pressure, gas, heat, load and rate excursions (pp. 1–5).


## SECTION 3: FAILURE MODES IDENTIFIED


### P01-F01: Low intake pressure / excessive drawdown

**Failure Mode:** Low intake pressure / excessive drawdown

**Category:** Reservoir

**Symptoms:** High rate with low PIP

**Root Causes:** Withdrawal relative to inflow; gas liberation is a candidate mechanism

**Consequences:** Loss of stable lift

**Typical Field Indicators:** PIP versus reported gas pressure

**Confidence Level:** Medium

**Evidence:** pp. 2–3

### P01-F02: Gas interference / gas locking

**Failure Mode:** Gas interference / gas locking

**Category:** Pump

**Symptoms:** Reduced throughput; potential inability to lift

**Root Causes:** Excess free gas at intake or inadequate separation

**Consequences:** Reduced or stopped production

**Typical Field Indicators:** Gas fraction, Turpin coefficient, lock ratio

**Confidence Level:** Medium

**Evidence:** pp. 2–3

### P01-F03: High-viscosity hydraulic impairment

**Confidence Level:** Medium

**Evidence:** p. 3

**Immediate Actions:** Validate rates, pressures and pump curve

**Short-Term Actions:** Review speed and gas handling with ESP specialist

**Long-Term Actions:** Reassess pump selection/separation
